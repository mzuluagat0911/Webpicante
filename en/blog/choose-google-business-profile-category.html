Your Google Business Profile Category Decides If You Show Up on Maps (And How to Reverse-Engineer Your Competitor's)

Your primary category is the single most important field for Google Maps relevance: it tells Google what search queries you can even compete for. To choose it, find the businesses ranking #1–#3 in your area, read their primary category, and match yours to the most specific accurate option (e.g. "Neapolitan pizza restaurant" over "Restaurant"). Then add up to 9 secondary categories for the other real services you offer—never fake ones, which can trigger suspension.

By Pulse · 2026-08-29 · 4 min read

If you're not showing up on Google Maps and you can't figure out why, start with the field almost everyone gets wrong: your category. Your Google Business Profile category is the setting that tells Google what your business actually is—and therefore which searches you're allowed to appear in. A coffee shop filed under "Restaurant" instead of "Coffee shop" is invisible for the exact queries its customers type. In dense markets like Miami, Mexico City, or São Paulo, where dozens of businesses fight for the same block, the wrong category is often the difference between page one and nowhere.

What a Google Business Profile category actually does

A category is Google's classification of your business type. It's not decoration—it's a ranking and eligibility signal. It controls three things at once:

  • Relevance: which search terms you're eligible to rank for. "Bakery" and "Brunch restaurant" surface you in different searches.
  • Features: the buttons, attributes, and menu fields your profile shows. A "Restaurant" category unlocks reservations and menu links; a "Retail" category unlocks different attributes.
  • Competitive set: who Google compares you against in the local pack.

Primary category vs secondary category is the distinction that matters most. The primary category carries the most weight for ranking—it's your main identity. Secondary categories broaden your reach into related services without diluting your core. You can add up to 9 secondary categories (10 total including the primary).

How to reverse-engineer your competitor's category

If a rival keeps winning your zone, don't guess—read their setup. Here's the step-by-step method:

  1. Run the search your customers run. Type the query that should show your business ("pizza near me," "brunch [neighborhood]") from a device in your area. Note who ranks #1–#3.
  2. Open each competitor's profile. Their primary category is displayed right under the business name on the profile.
  3. Deduce their secondary categories. Google doesn't publish these directly, but you can infer them from the buttons, attributes, and services shown (order online, reservations, "serves brunch," "outdoor seating"). Each feature hints at an underlying category.
  4. Map the gap. Compare their primary to yours. If the #1 result is "Neapolitan pizza restaurant" and you're just "Restaurant," that's your gap. Specific beats generic every time when the specific term is accurate.
  5. Adjust with judgment. Change your primary only if the more specific category genuinely describes your core business. Then fill secondaries with your real additional services.

How to choose your category (and which restaurant categories to use)

The rule is simple: be the most specific category that is still 100% true. Google rewards precision. A few examples from the Google Business Profile category list for restaurants:

  • Italian restaurant, Neapolitan pizza restaurant, Pizza restaurant
  • Coffee shop, Café, Espresso bar
  • Bakery, Pastry shop, Bagel shop
  • Brunch restaurant, Breakfast restaurant, Diner
  • Taco restaurant, Mexican restaurant, Taqueria

If your business does several things—say café + bakery + brunch—pick the one that represents most of your revenue and identity as the primary, and add the rest as secondaries. If people primarily come to you for coffee, "Coffee shop" is primary and "Bakery" and "Brunch restaurant" are secondary.

The mistakes that cost you the zone

  • Staying generic. "Restaurant" when you're clearly a "Sushi restaurant" throws away relevance for your best queries.
  • Inflating with fake categories. Adding categories you don't actually serve to grab more searches is a fast track to suspension. Only list what you truly offer.
  • Ignoring features. The wrong category hides the buttons customers need—reservations, ordering, menu. This quietly kills conversions.
  • Set-and-forget. Google adds and renames categories regularly. A more specific option may have appeared since you last checked. Review every quarter, and any time your offering changes.

Does changing your category affect ranking? Yes—and that's the point. Moving to a more accurate, specific category typically helps relevance. It does not delete your reviews or reset your history; reviews stay attached to the profile. But changing back and forth carelessly signals instability, so change deliberately, not on a whim.

The multi-location category check

Running several branches? Same criteria, applied per location—not blindly copied. In general, locations of the same brand should share the same primary category for consistency and brand signal. But secondaries should reflect what each branch actually offers: a location with a full brunch service adds "Brunch restaurant," one without it doesn't. A replicable ritual for chains:

  1. Define the brand-standard primary category once.
  2. Audit each branch's secondaries against its real menu and services.
  3. Compare every branch against the local #1–#3 in its own zone—competition differs by neighborhood.
  4. Re-check quarterly and log changes so ops and owners see the same criteria.

Category is the cheapest, fastest lever you have on Maps. Get it right, keep it accurate, and stop handing Google a reason to rank someone else in your zone.

Frequently asked questions

Does the primary category weigh more than secondaries for Maps ranking?

Yes. The primary category is the strongest category signal for Google Maps relevance—it defines your core business identity and the main queries you're eligible to rank for. Secondary categories extend your reach into related services but carry less weight, so choose your primary carefully.

How many secondary categories can I add?

You can add up to 9 secondary categories, for a maximum of 10 categories total including your primary. Only list categories that accurately describe services you actually offer—adding false ones can trigger a suspension.

Can I see which category my competitor uses?

Yes, partially. A business's primary category is shown publicly right under its name on its Google Business Profile. Secondary categories aren't published, but you can infer them from the buttons, attributes and services displayed on the profile.

Does changing my category reset my ranking or reviews?

Changing to a more accurate, specific category usually helps relevance rather than hurting it, and it does not delete your reviews or history—they stay attached to the profile. Avoid switching back and forth frequently, though, since erratic changes signal instability.

What category do I choose if my business does several things, like café plus bakery plus brunch?

Pick the option that represents most of your revenue and identity as the primary category, then add the others as secondaries. If people mainly come for coffee, set "Coffee shop" as primary and add "Bakery" and "Brunch restaurant" as secondaries.

In multi-location businesses, should all branches have the same category?

Locations of the same brand should generally share the same primary category for consistency, but secondary categories should reflect what each branch actually offers. Also compare each branch to the top competitors in its own neighborhood, since the local competitive set varies by zone.
