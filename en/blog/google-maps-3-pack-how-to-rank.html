How to Get Into the Google Maps 3-Pack: What It Is and How to Rank

The Google Maps 3-pack (also called the local pack) is the block of three businesses Google shows at the top of local search results, above the regular list. Appearing on Maps is not the same as being in the 3-pack: Maps lists everyone, the 3-pack shows only the three winners for that query and location. To get in, you optimize the three factors Google uses—relevance, distance and prominence—especially rating, reviews, review responses and profile activity.

By Pulse · 2026-09-26 · 4 min read

Your customers search for you on Google before they ever walk in. But being visible on Google Maps and winning your area are two different things. Plenty of business owners see their pin on the map and assume they're competitive—until they realize a rival is sitting in the top 3 and eating the calls, directions and walk-ins. This guide separates "showing up on Maps" from "getting into the 3-pack," and gives you a concrete way to compete for it.

What the Google Maps 3-pack is (and why it matters)

The local pack, or 3-pack, is the group of exactly three business listings Google displays at the top of a local search, right under the map. It's the most valuable real estate in local search: it appears before the organic blue links and before the scrollable list of other businesses.

The difference between appearing on Google Maps and appearing in the local pack is simple but critical:

  • On Maps: your business exists, has a pin, and can be found if someone scrolls or searches your name directly.
  • In the 3-pack: Google actively recommends you as one of the top three answers for a category search like "tacos near me" or "dentist downtown."

Around 90% of people check Google before choosing a local business. If you're on Maps but not in the top 3, you're visible but not chosen—which is why owners ask, "why does my business show on Maps but not in the top 3?" The answer is almost always ranking, not existence.

The 3 factors that decide the pack: what you control vs. what you don't

Google ranks local results using three factors. Understanding which ones you can move is how you stop guessing.

1. Relevance (you control this a lot)

How well your profile matches what the person searched. Levers you control: your primary and secondary categories, business name, services, description and the words that appear in your reviews. A restaurant that never sets "Mexican restaurant" as a category won't rank for it, no matter how good the food is.

2. Distance (you control this the least)

How far your location is from where the searcher is standing. You can't move your storefront, but you can understand that the pack is dynamic and location-based—the same search shows different top-3 results two blocks apart. This is the single most misunderstood part of local ranking.

3. Prominence (you control this daily)

How well-known and trusted your business is. This is where most of the fight happens: rating, number of reviews, freshness of reviews, whether you respond to them, and profile activity like photos and posts. Two identical taquerías on the same corner will rank differently based almost entirely on prominence.

The 20-minute checklist to compete for the 3-pack

You don't need a month-long project. Do this in one sitting:

  1. Fix your categories. Set the most accurate primary category, then add every relevant secondary category.
  2. Check your rating and volume. Compare yourself to the three businesses currently in the pack for your main keyword. If they have more (and fresher) reviews, that's your gap.
  3. Respond to every recent review. Responses are a signal Google reads and a trust signal customers read. Reply to the last 10, good and bad.
  4. Add fresh photos. Upload 5–10 recent, real photos of the space, product and team.
  5. Verify consistency. Name, address, phone and hours must match everywhere.
  6. Search from your customer's location. Check the 3-pack from the actual streets around you, not just your office Wi-Fi.

Common mistakes that keep you out of the pack

  • Assuming Maps visibility = winning. Being findable by name means nothing if you lose the category search.
  • Ignoring review responses. A profile with dozens of unanswered reviews signals a business that isn't paying attention.
  • Checking rank from one spot. The pack changes by neighborhood; a single check gives you a false read.
  • Chasing review quantity while ignoring recency. Fifty reviews from three years ago lose to fifteen from this month.
  • Treating every branch the same. Each location competes in its own zone against different rivals.

Competing for the pack with multiple locations

If you run several branches, the game changes: the 3-pack is decided zone by zone. Your downtown location might dominate while your second store loses its pack to a local competitor you've never heard of. You can't manage that with one dashboard and one gut feeling.

The multi-location approach: treat each branch as its own battle. Track the actual top-3 competitors around each store, watch their rating and review trend, and keep review responses flowing per location—so one weak branch doesn't quietly bleed customers while the flagship shines.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Google local pack?

The local pack is the block of three business listings Google shows at the top of a local search, right under the map. It's also called the 3-pack, and it appears above the organic links and the longer list of businesses, making it the most visible spot in local search.

How many businesses appear in the 3-pack?

Exactly three. Google shows only three businesses in the local pack for a given search and location, which is why competition for those spots is intense—everyone else gets pushed below the fold or into the expanded list.

Why do I appear in search but not in the highlighted map results?

Because appearing on Maps just means your business exists and can be found, while the 3-pack means Google recommends you as a top answer. If you're visible but not in the top 3, the issue is ranking—usually weaker relevance (categories) or prominence (rating, reviews, responses) than competitors.

Does the 3-pack change based on where the user is?

Yes. The local pack is dynamic and location-based, so the same search can show different top-3 results just a few blocks apart. That's why you should always check your ranking from your customers' actual location, not just your office.

Can I pay to appear in the organic top 3?

No. The 3-pack organic positions can't be bought; they're earned through relevance, distance and prominence. Google does show paid local ads in some searches, but those are labeled and separate from the three organic pack results.

How do I compete for the pack if I have branches in several zones?

Treat each location as its own battle, because the 3-pack is decided zone by zone. Track the top-3 competitors around each branch separately, monitor their rating and review trend, and keep review responses active per location so no single branch quietly loses its area.
